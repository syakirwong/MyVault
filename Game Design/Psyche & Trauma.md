# Psyche & Trauma

Everyone aboard the *Halyard*, the captain included, has an inner life the game tracks: old wounds, current stress, ways of coping, and things they won't say. This is the main source of **unpredictability**. People act from what's inside them, not from what the player expects.

**Design rule: surprising in the moment, inevitable in hindsight.** Every surprise is foreshadowed by signals the player could have noticed. The [[Choice & Consequence#Feedback Rules|Consequence Ledger]] points them out afterwards.

## Psyche State (per character)

| State | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress** | 0-100 | Rises with danger, loss, conflict with you, and gem strain. Falls with rest, Dusk time, friendship, and small joys |
| **Wounds** | List | Old trauma, each with **triggers** (situations that reopen it) |
| **Imprints** | List | New trauma made during play: a death they saw, a jail cell, a betrayal |
| **Coping style** | Healthy or harmful | Talking, humour, work / drinking, rage, withdrawal, recklessness, clinging |
| **Secret** | One per character | Something they hide. It may come out under pressure |

Stress and [[Crew & Bonds#Gem Strain|gem strain]] **compound**. A stressed crew member strains their gem faster, and a straining gem frays their mind. A good relationship ([[Relationships & Life Events]]) is the strongest buffer.

## Breaking Points

When stress reaches its peak (**90+**), a character has a **breaking point** scene. What happens depends on their coping style and how they feel about you:

- **Breakdown:** they can't work for days, and need you at Dusk.
- **Lashing out:** a fight, a cruel truth, violence against crew or a stranger.
- **Flight:** they jump ship at the next port, or vanish mid-call.
- **Revelation:** their secret comes out, sometimes at the worst moment.
- **Resolve:** rarely, and only with a strong bond, they come through stronger, and their wound changes into something they can carry.

## The Captain's Mind

The captain has a psyche too, built from what *you* chose:

- **Guilt ledger:** people you failed or sacrificed. They come back in dreams and **flashback panels** at bad moments.
- **The core's whisper:** the more you use it, the more its voice blends into the captain's own thoughts. Some narration panels are the core speaking, and the game doesn't always say which.
- **Unreliable log:** at high stress or deep denial, the Captain's Log can be *wrong*: it softens what you did, or blames someone else. Later scenes and other characters contradict it.

## Presentation

| Device | Look |
|---|---|
| **Flashback panel** | Inverted colours (white on ink), flickering, jagged edges; breaks into the present scene without warning |
| **Intrusive thought** | A small panel in the captain's voice that you can't skip past, only read |
| **Unreliable narration** | A caption that later gets a red correction stamped over it ("That isn't what happened.") |
| **Dissociation** | Panels drain to grey, and choices blur and become fewer |
| **Calm after** | One quiet, wordless panel: the sea, a shared cup, a hand on a shoulder |

## Handling It Well

1. **People, not meters.** Stress numbers are never shown. Players read it from behaviour, dialogue and the Ledger.
2. **No "madness" mechanics.** Mental illness is not a debuff or a monster. Oriel's voices stay ambiguous and are treated with dignity.
3. **No magic cures.** Love doesn't fix trauma. Support helps people carry it.
4. **Recovery is possible and earned:** honest conversations, rest, being believed, time.
5. **Suicide and self-harm** are referenced only with care, never shown, and always flagged by content notes ([[Tone & Content#Rating and Content Controls]]). Settings include a page of real-world support resources.

# Story Bible

The source of truth for **what actually happened**, who the captain is, and which threads are live when Episode 1 begins. The world's history comes from [[Lore & Narrative]]; this note sets the parts the narrative game depends on. The playable branches are in [[Episode 1 Decision Tree]].

> [!warning] Writers only
> Sections marked **Hidden truth** are never stated outright in the game. Players piece them together from Lattice Memories, songs, objects and confessions ([[Story Structure#Lore in the Story]]). Factions tell their own versions, and every version is partly right.

---

## 1. The World the Player Wakes Into

What every dockhand in Port Haven believes:

- Long ago the land was whole, a continent called **the Convergence**. Then the **Shattering** broke it into the archipelago and broke the **Attunement Lattice** into gems.
- The **Perpetual Gale** never ends. At its heart is the **storm wall**, a ring of weather no ship comes back from.
- Gems are in everything. A few people are bonded to one. Push a gem too hard and it **strains**, then mutates, then breaks, and the person is **Quieted** ([[Peoples, Clans & Cast#The Peoples]]).
- Three great powers argue about what to do with what's left: the **Luminari** (restore it), the **Hollow Court** (feed the change), and the **Driftborn** (let it choose).
- Port Haven is neutral ground under the **Trade Guild**, and Guild law says **whoever lifts a thing from the sea owns it**. That's salvage rights, and it's the only law everyone respects.

## 2. Hidden Truth: What the Lattice Was

**The Lattice was a mind made of the dead.**

In the Age of Resonance, the dead weren't buried. The **Choir**, the singers of the Convergence and the ancestors of the Tidefolk, sang each dying person *into* the Lattice. Every mind joined the whole, and the dead held the world in balance. Nobody was ever truly gone, so nobody truly grieved.

Over thousands of years the Lattice filled. The dead couldn't rest, and the living stopped changing. The greatest node, a mind the legends call **the Tidecaller**, asked to be let go. The Choir refused and tried to bind the Lattice tighter, to make it hold *forever*.

The Lattice broke itself rather than be held.

| Faction's telling | What's true in it | What it leaves out |
|---|---|---|
| **Luminari:** hubris broke it | The Choir's attempt to bind it forever triggered the break | That restoring it means re-imprisoning the dead |
| **Hollow Court:** it was inevitable | A closed system of the dead could never hold | That the "change" they feed is the dead waking up in pieces |
| **Driftborn:** it chose | It did choose, through the Tidecaller | That choosing destroyed a continent and drowned millions |

**What this means in play:**

| Thing in the world | What it really is |
|---|---|
| Gems | Splinters of the dead, carrying a domain and a faint remnant of a mind |
| Gem strain and mutation | The remnant waking and pushing back |
| The Quieted | People whose gem-remnant went silent, which is why they feel muted |
| Oriel's "Void voices" | The dead, heard through the Void gem. She isn't ill. She's listening |
| Tidefolk songs | The Choir's funeral songs. They're Lattice memories because they were the means of entry |
| Wakened constructs | Cores holding enough of one mind to become a person again |
| The core in the Stormspire | A **Keystone**, one of the few fragments that kept a whole mind. This one holds the Tidecaller |

The season's final question grows out of this: **is the core a person, and if so, who gets to decide what happens to it?**

## 3. Hidden Truth: The Stormspire (30 years before Episode 1)

The **Stormspire** was a Luminari reliquary ship carrying the Keystone from a vault to the High Luminar for a **restoration rite**.

1. Its captain, **Ilse Carrow**, touched the Keystone and heard it. It told her what the rite would do: bind it again, forever.
2. Ilse made a **blood pact** with the core ([[Lineage, Pacts & Diplomacy#Blood Pacts]]). Her words: *"I'll take you where no one can use you."*
3. She turned the ship toward the Drift. Her infant child was aboard.
4. Her first officer, **Aldous Sallow**, signalled the escort cutter. The cutter fired on the Stormspire at the edge of the storm wall, and the ship went down.
5. **Bosun Marlo** was in the water with Ilse and the baby. He believes he let go of her hand to save himself. The truth, held in a Lattice Memory in the wreck: **Ilse let go of him, and pushed the baby into his arms.**
6. Marlo, a Calm Shores man, set the baby adrift in a **funeral lamp-float** (the Calm Shores rite, [[Lineage, Pacts & Diplomacy#Traditions and Culture]]) so the searching cutter would take it for an offering and leave it be. The tide carried it to Port Haven.
7. Marlo quit the navy within the year. He has watched the child grow up from a distance ever since.

**That child is the captain.** The pact Ilse made is unfinished, and it's in the captain's blood. That's why the core recognises them ([[Lineage, Pacts & Diplomacy#Family Lineage]]).

**Eighteen years before Episode 1**, Sallow, now Hollow Court and grey-handed with mutation, hired the **Vey clan** to pilot into the storm wall and find the wreck. Their ship, the *Kestrel*, went in and didn't come out. Tamsin, aged six, watched it go from a tender.

**Three months before Episode 1**, the storm wall pulled back about twenty miles. The Stormspire and the *Kestrel* now lie on a reef at its edge, reachable for the first time in thirty years. Oriel started hearing a new voice that same week.

## 4. The Captain

### Who You Are (every playthrough)

- **Found, not born.** You were pulled out of a funeral lamp-float on the Port Haven tide about thirty years ago. Nobody claimed you.
- **The Lamp** is your one possession from before: a brass Calm Shores funeral lamp with a house sigil scratched off its base, all but one corner. It's your starting **Keepsake** ([[Gear, Relics & Reliquaries#Gear Slots (per crew member)]]).
- **No gem.** Gems hum when you pass. You thought everyone heard it.
- **Wound:** *Nobody came for you.* **Trigger:** being left behind, or someone leaving.
- **What you want at the start:** a ship of your own, so you'll never again depend on someone deciding to keep you.

### Customisation

Resolves the GDD open question: **name, pronouns, and one of three backgrounds.** The background changes some options, a few lore keys, and one relationship. It never locks you out of an ending.

| Background | Where Port Haven put you | Unlocks | Starting relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lamp House** | Raised in the Guild foundling house, worked the docks from age nine | Knows Harbormaster Finn, dock smuggling routes, Guild salvage law (a lore key at the finale) | **Nana**: she paid for the Lamp House roof. She's known your face for years |
| **Struck Cadet** | Sponsored into the Luminari naval academy by an anonymous donor, then expelled for striking an instructor | Luminari scripture and oath words (a lore key on day 5). **Inspector Valk** was in your year | **Hesk** distrusts you on sight: you walk like a patrol |
| **Bought Debt** | The foundling house sold your debt to Ironvale at twelve. You worked it off in the pit-yards | Ironvale cant and debt-ink; you can read a debt contract (a lore key on day 1) | **Brannoc**: you fought on the same card once. He remembers |

The anonymous donor in **Struck Cadet** was Marlo. In **Lamp House**, the roof money was Marlo's, passed through Nana. In **Bought Debt**, Marlo tried to buy your debt and was outbid. Every background has Marlo's hand in it, and every one can be found out.

### The Captain's Arc (season)

| Episode | The captain learns | The question it raises |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The core knows them. The Stormspire was sunk, not wrecked | *Why me?* |
| 2 | Their mother was Ilse Carrow. Marlo carried them out | *Do I forgive him?* |
| 3 | What gems are, from inside a feeding | *What am I carrying?* |
| 4 | The exact words of Ilse's pact | *Do I owe a promise I never made?* |
| 5 | What the Tidecaller wants | *Keep my mother's oath, or break it?* |

## 5. The Core

- **Look:** a fist-sized Lattice core, the grey of a storm sky, warm, with a pulse slower than a heartbeat.
- **Voice:** at first it doesn't speak in words. It sends feelings and fragments of memory to whoever carries it. By Episode 1's end it has a few words, borrowed from the people around it. It speaks in **their phrasing**, which is how players notice it learning.
- **What it calls the captain:** *"the lamp."*
- **Personality:** curious, old, and grieving. It doesn't yet know whether it's the Tidecaller or something new grown from the pieces. Its **alignment** (Radiant, Corrupted, Unbound) is what it learns from how the captain treats people ([[Choice & Consequence#State Model]]).
- **The players name it.** On day 4 the crew argue about what to call it. The name you choose sticks for the season, and some names change how certain crew speak to it.

## 8. Story Complexities

### Threads, Seeds and Payoffs

| Thread | Seeded in Ep 1 | Can surface in Ep 1 | Pays off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The captain's lineage** | The Lamp's sigil; the ring on the bones in the captain's cabin; the Sentinel's greeting | The sigil match (a Tell panel) if you take the ring | Ep 2 |
| **Marlo was on the Stormspire** | His refusal on day 1; he knows the Sentinel's name; the logbook | Partial confession on day 3 or at Dusk | Ep 2, full truth |
| **Sallow sank the ship** | Marlo's reaction to him on day 1 | Valk's records on day 5 (Dawn Schism route) | Ep 3 |
| **The Kestrel** | Tamsin's charts | Day 3, if she boards it: the sextant case is empty | Ep 2-3: Sallow has the Vey Storm-Sextant |
| **Hesk and Brisk** | Hesk brokers the loan | Read the contract, or Brannoc tells you at Dusk | Ep 1 day 6, or Ep 3 |
| **Nana is dying** | She's selling the ship | Oriel's medicines; Nana's Dusk at bond 2 | Ep 2-4, her Path ([[Professions & Advancement#The Crew's Paths]]) |
| **Oriel is half-Tidefolk** | Syl's line on day 2 | Not in Ep 1 | Ep 4 |
| **Marlo served on purification patrols** | The Stormspire logbook has his signature | If Hesk reads the log | Ep 2-3, Marlo ↔ Hesk |
| **The core is learning** | Its borrowed words | Every day: whose phrasing it uses | Season |

### Clocks

| Clock | Runs out | If it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The debt** | Day 7 | Brisk sells your debt to Sallow, or the Court collects in person |
| **Nana's illness** | Across the season | She dies before her arc ends unless it's slowed by medicine, rest, or the core |
| **Core stability** | Starts at 70 | Below 30 it acts on its own and overrides the finale ([[Episode 1 Decision Tree#Endings]]) |
| **Marlo's stress** | Rises every day near the wreck | Breaking point at the wreck: he confesses, or he runs |

### Moral Pressure Points

Each one has no right answer. Every option is defended by at least one crew member ([[Choice & Consequence#Writing Rules]]).

1. **Money or mercy:** the tow, and the debt deadline.
2. **Truth or safety:** telling the Luminari, and telling the crew.
3. **Whose voice counts:** the vote, and whether the core gets one.
4. **Inheritance:** a promise your mother made, which you never agreed to.
5. **The person in the box:** whether the core is cargo or crew.

## 9. Prologue: "The Lamp"

The cold open plays before character creation. About 90 seconds, no choices.

> **[Flashback panel, white on ink]** Black water. Lightning. A hand in a hand.
>
> **[Flashback panel]** The fingers open. Whose, you can't tell.
>
> **[Caption]** *Something small, floating. A light inside it.*
>
> **[Impact, hard cut]** SILENCE.
>
> **[Caption, present]** *Port Haven. Thirty years later. The harbour gems are humming again, and only you seem to hear them.*
>
> **[Speech] HARBORMASTER FINN:** Name for the ledger, Captain?
>
> **[Caption]** *You've never been called that before.*

Character creation follows as Finn's ledger: **name**, **pronouns**, and *"Where did Port Haven put you?"* (the background). Then Day 1 begins.

The flashback is replayed in Episode 2 with the hands shown clearly: Ilse lets go.
